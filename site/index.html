A few things
you might wonder.

How do I install it?

Download the DMG, open it, and drag Chirpberry into Applications. Or use the Homebrew command above. This early release is not notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks it and you choose to trust the app, follow Apple's instructions. Add your Valsea API key in Settings before using speech or summaries.

Is it free?

The Mac app and source code are free under the MIT license. Manual notes, local search, and exports work without a Valsea key. Valsea bills speech, translation, and summary processing to your own account. Microphone plus Mac audio uses two streams; optional speaker detection adds provider cost.

Where does my meeting go?

Your saved notes and transcripts stay on your Mac. During recording, audio streams directly to Valsea. Requested summaries also use Valsea. Chirpberry doesn't store audio recordings or run its own cloud storage. Valsea's policies apply to its processing. Let participants know before transcribing a conversation.

Which languages can I translate into?

Live targets include English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai, French, Spanish, German, Russian, Indonesian, Malay, Filipino, Tamil, Khmer, and Lao. Source-language and speaker-detection coverage vary. Original captions arrive first; translation arrives with a completed segment.

Does it work with my meeting app?

Chirpberry captures microphone and optional system audio through macOS rather than joining the call. You grant microphone and Screen & System Audio Recording permissions when needed. In-person voice notes can use the microphone alone.

Can I ask questions across my notes?

Local search works independently of AI. When Apple Intelligence is enabled and its on-device model is ready, Chirpberry can answer using retrieved meeting excerpts and source references. You can also connect the bundled read-only MCP server to your own AI client.
